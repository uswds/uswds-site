## Start with a community discussion

Anyone can suggest a component or pattern in a proposal discussion. Early ideas are welcome; you do not need every answer before starting. Search existing discussions first and add evidence to a related discussion where possible.

The proposer and community develop the case for the idea. Work together to answer:

- **Why is it needed?** Describe the user need, what is missing from USWDS, and any relevant requirements.
- **How does it work?** Describe interactions, states, variations, and relationships with existing components or patterns.
- **Where is it used?** Link examples from real government services and explain what teams have learned.
- **When should it be used?** Describe appropriate contexts, alternatives, and situations where it would not help.
- **What supports its usability?** Share research and identify questions that still need investigation.
- **What supports its accessibility?** Describe keyboard, screen reader, zoom, voice input, and other relevant interactions. Distinguish tested results from assumptions and open questions.
- **Who can contribute?** Identify people or teams who can provide research, design, development, testing, or documentation.

Share what you have and make missing evidence explicit. A working implementation can help the discussion, but does not by itself establish that an idea fits the design system.

## When the core team participates

The core team may ask questions or offer feedback during discussion. This does not mean the idea has been approved or scheduled. Community members can continue gathering evidence without waiting for the team to join every conversation.

When the discussion addresses the proposal questions, summarize the evidence and remaining gaps in that discussion. The core team considers the case alongside other priorities and its capacity to support the work. A complete set of answers or a high vote count does not guarantee a formal proposal, a release date, or a response within a fixed period.

The status changes to **Proposal in progress** when the core team begins drafting a formal proposal. The core team coordinates that proposal, shares it for a minimum 45-day comment period, and evaluates feedback. Keep questions and comments in the original public discussion so the community can follow the decision.

Approval and assignment are separate lifecycle steps. An approved proposal can remain unassigned until the people and resources needed to develop it are available. See the [component lifecycle](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/lifecycle/) for the later stages.
